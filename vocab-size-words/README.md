# Size words vocabulary

Study game for Sam, 7th grade. The list is built on shared Latin and Greek roots: micro, macro, mega, magn, mini/minu, poly, and ten, plus the words that sit beside them.

Open it from the science game's home page, or go straight to `vocab-size-words/` on GitHub Pages: https://brookhouser.github.io/sam-science-game/vocab-size-words/

Modes: flashcards, word-to-definition multiple choice, definition-to-word multiple choice, Quick Match, a 60-second speed round, fill-in-the-blank sentences, a roots quiz, missed items, a study guide, Test Prep, and Defend the Microcosm. Correct answers in Defend the Microcosm earn coins. Coins buy towers that stop the mega march before it reaches the tiny world.

Answer choices are shuffled. The meanings in the game are the definitions below, word for word. Sentence questions use those words. They do not add new vocabulary words.

## The 19 words

- **Attenuate:** To reduce the force, effect, thickness, or value of something; to weaken or make slender.
- **Comply:** To act in accordance with a wish, request, rule, or command.
- **Expletive:** An oath or swear word; in grammar, a word or phrase that fills out a sentence without adding meaning (such as "it" or "there" in empty subject positions).
- **Implement:** To put a decision, plan, or agreement into effect; as a noun, a tool or instrument used for a specific task.
- **Microbe:** A microscopic living organism, especially a bacterium, virus, or fungus, particularly one that causes disease or fermentation.
- **Microcosm:** A community, place, or situation regarded as encapsulating in miniature the characteristic qualities or features of something much larger.
- **Minuscule:** Extremely small or tiny.
- **Minutia:** Precise, trivial, or minor details (commonly used in the plural, minutiae).
- **Replete:** Filled or well-supplied with something; gorged or full to satisfaction, especially with food and drink.
- **Satiate:** To satisfy a desire, appetite, or need fully, often to the point of excess or boredom.
- **Tenuous:** Very weak, slight, or flimsy; having little substance or basis.
- **Copious:** Abundant in supply, quantity, or volume; plentiful.
- **Macrocosm:** The whole of a complex structure, such as the universe or society, contrasted with a smaller or representative part of it.
- **Magnanimous:** Generous, forgiving, and noble in spirit, especially toward a rival or someone less powerful.
- **Magnate:** A wealthy, influential, and powerful person, especially in a specific business or industry (such as a real estate or oil tycoon).
- **Magnitude:** The great size, extent, importance, or scale of something; in science, a numerical measure of brightness or earthquake energy.
- **Megalomania:** An obsession with the exercise of power, especially in the domination of others; a delusional belief in one's own greatness or importance.
- **Polygamy:** The practice or custom of having more than one spouse at the same time.
- **Polygon:** A flat, two-dimensional geometric shape with at least three straight sides and angles.

## Two senses, and what the game will not say

- **Expletive** is practiced in both senses. The oath sense is named as an oath or swear word. The game never shows an actual swear word. The grammar sense uses "it" and "there", as in "It is snowing" and "There are two pizzas on the table."
- **Implement** is practiced in both senses. The verb means to put a decision, plan, or agreement into effect. The noun is a tool or instrument used for a specific task, such as a whisk or a hoe.
- **Polygamy** stays at the plain definition above. The questions do not add anything beyond that sentence.

## Roots

- **micro** means small: microbe, microcosm.
- **macro** means large: macrocosm.
- **mega** means great: megalomania.
- **magn** means great: magnanimous, magnate, magnitude. Mega and magn both relate to great, and they are different roots.
- **mini / minu** means small: minuscule, minutia.
- **poly** means many: polygon, polygamy.
- **ten** means thin: attenuate, tenuous.

Comply, expletive, implement, and replete also connect to Latin words about filling or fulfilling. Satiate comes from satis, enough. Copious comes from copia, plenty. The roots quiz includes those notes.
